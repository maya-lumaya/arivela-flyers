# Concept #4 — "Off the Center"

> **Big idea / territory:** the same ARIVELA brand family, but a deliberately **asymmetric, off-center** layout language. Where concepts 1–3 are *centered* — a centred constellation, a centred editorial cover, a centred stacked toolkit — this one breaks the symmetry on purpose. A strong **off-center vertical axis** runs down one side, big quiet **negative space** opens on the other, type sets **against an edge**, and the five geographic areas (Rishikesh · Ubud · London · Berlin · Tulum) live **at the margins** — anchored to an edge, dropping diagonally down a rail, scattering toward a corner, or flowing off the page. It feels like a modern art-magazine spread: a gallery wall where the work is hung *off* center and the white space is part of the composition. Same palette, same Lora/Inter, same quiet-invitation voice, same photography rules — a clearly new *layout language* versus the first three.

---

## 1. Positioning

**The one idea:** *the world isn't centred, and neither are we.* ARIVELA is a community that lives **at the edges of the map** — scattered across five cities in four countries, off any one center — and the flyer's own composition embodies that. The places don't gather into a tidy ring in the middle of the page; they sit at the **margins**, the way a real community is spread out, decentralised, plural. The reader feels a calm, confident, *curated-gallery* energy: nothing is begging for the center of attention, and that restraint is the sophistication.

**How the off-center / asymmetric idea is expressed (the defining move):**

- **A single off-center axis, not a centered stack.** Each side is built on one strong vertical line placed roughly at the **1/3 (golden) position**, not the middle. On the FRONT the axis sits ~62% across (content weight on the **left**, an open field of cream on the **right**). On the BACK it **mirrors** to ~38% (content weight on the **right**, open field on the **left**) — so flipping the flyer flips the asymmetry, and the two sides read as a matched, intentional pair rather than two random off-center pages.
- **Type set against an edge.** The Lora-italic hero is **ranged hard to one vertical edge** of the live area (FRONT: ranged left, hugging the left safe-margin; BACK: ranged right, hugging the right safe-margin), with the big negative space falling to the opposite side — the way a gallery hangs a title placard flush to a wall and lets the room breathe beside it.
- **A diagonal counter-axis.** A quiet implied diagonal (top-anchored on the content side, sweeping toward the opposite bottom corner) gives the composition movement and keeps it from feeling like a centred column shoved sideways. The photo frame / duotone art is clipped so it **bleeds off one edge** (never fully contained, never centred), reinforcing "off the page, into the world."
- **Negative space is a deliberate element**, not leftover. The empty cream field on the open side is sized and shaped on purpose — it carries the eye, holds one or two off-center area-labels, and lets the composition feel like an art spread, not a crowded leaflet.

**Where the five city-areas sit OFF-CENTER on each side (the signature of the concept):**

- **FRONT (guests):** the five areas run as a **vertical "edge rail" down the right margin**, stepping **diagonally downward** (a gentle staircase from upper-right toward lower-right), each city a small Lora cap with a tiny node-dot on the safe-margin line — so the "map" of places lives **at the right edge of the page, off-center, descending**, never a centred footer line and never a centred constellation. One or two cities sit slightly *further* off (kerned toward the very margin) so the rail feels scattered, not ruler-straight. The worldwide-community payoff line tucks in **low-left**, diagonally opposite the rail.
- **BACK (hosts):** the rail **mirrors to the left margin** and steps the other way (a staircase descending from upper-left toward lower-left), the five cities as small Lora caps with node-dots hugging the left safe-margin. So across the two sides the area-rail **switches edges** — right on the front, left on the back — making the asymmetry obviously deliberate and tying the spine across both sides while never centring it.

**Why this is distinct from the other three concepts:**

- **Not Concept 1 ("The Worldwide Circle").** That concept's hero is a **centred constellation / ring** of the five places, dead center as the focal artwork, with a centred five-city footer framing the page top-and-bottom. Concept 4 deliberately **refuses the centred ring**: the same five places are **strung along an edge as a descending rail**, the composition is asymmetric, and the negative space — not the constellation — is the second protagonist.
- **Not Concept 2 ("People You Can Trust").** That one is a **centred fine-magazine cover** — a single quiet sentence and frame held in symmetric calm, cities as a thin **centred footnote** at the foot. Concept 4 keeps the restraint and the cream, but throws the weight **off-axis**: type flush to an edge, art bleeding off the opposite edge, cities running **up an edge rail**, not along the bottom.
- **Not Concept 3 ("Your Practice Has a Home").** That one is a **centred / stacked toolkit** — a tidy vertical rhythm of centred cards and a centred footer spine. Concept 4 is the **anti-stack**: an off-center axis, a diagonal counter-movement, and an edge-anchored area rail. Where 3 feels like neatly shelved order, 4 feels like a curated gallery wall hung off-center.

**Distinct feeling in one breath:** the other three feel *composed around a center*. This one feels *composed around an edge and an open field* — modern, confident, a little more art-directed; a community that lives at the margins of the map, presented like a gallery spread.

## 4. Layout skeleton per side — the ASYMMETRIC grid

Page = `1748 × 2480 px`, full-bleed cream background, all text + QR + wordmark inside the ~118px (10mm) safe margin. Both the photo builder and the duotone builder follow **this exact asymmetric grid** so the photo pair and the duotone pair each converge as one piece; the **only** thing that swaps is what fills the "art zone" (a styled photo frame vs. an inline-SVG illustration). The defining structural rule shared by both treatments: **one off-center vertical axis + one big negative-space field + the area-rail anchored to a margin edge + an implied diagonal — never a centred column.**

**The off-center axis (the spine of the grid):**
- FRONT axis at **~62% width** (≈1085px from left): content sits **left of it**, open cream field sits **right of it**. The area rail rides the **far right safe-margin**.
- BACK axis at **~38% width** (≈665px from left): content sits **right of it**, open cream field sits **left of it**. The area rail rides the **far left safe-margin**.
- Mirroring the axis front↔back is what makes the asymmetry read as deliberate craft.

### FRONT (guests) — content-left, open-field-right, area-rail down the right edge

1. **Masthead / wordmark** — **top-left**, flush to the left safe-margin (NOT centred). `ARIVELA` (Inter 600, uppercase, ~0.4em tracking, `#1D1D21`) with the 2px orange accent rule **left-aligned beneath it** (not centered). The eyebrow `FROM EVERYWHERE, FOR ANYONE` sits just under it in orange, also left-flush. *(top band, ~10% height)*
2. **Hero zone** — the Lora-italic hero **ranged hard left**, hugging the left edge, three lines, large, generous leading; subhead in Inter directly beneath, also left. The big **negative-space cream field opens to the right** of the hero — deliberately empty, holding only the top of the area rail. This empty field is the composition's second protagonist. *(~24%)*
3. **Art zone (photo-or-illustration)** — a **tall, off-center frame** placed left-of-axis and **bleeding off the LEFT edge** (clipped at the left bleed, never fully contained, never centred). It's portrait-ish, anchored low-left, with its top sitting under the hero and its right edge stopping at the off-center axis so the right field stays open. In photo treatment this is a styled empty frame with art direction; in duotone it's the inline SVG. *(~30%, but shaped as a left-bleeding block, not a full-width band)*
4. **The area rail (the off-center signature)** — the five cities run **down the right safe-margin as a descending diagonal staircase**: `Rishikesh` highest (upper-right, just below the masthead band), then `Ubud`, `London`, `Berlin`, `Tulum` stepping down and a hair further toward the right edge, each a small Lora cap (~30–34px) with a tiny orange node-dot on the margin line and a faint connecting hairline so it reads as one rail. One or two cities kern slightly further out so the rail feels scattered, not ruled. The rail spans from the hero band down to the lower third — living entirely **at the right edge, off-center**. *(occupies the right margin column, ~top-25%→bottom-30%)*
5. **Worldwide-community + supporting copy** — set **low-left**, diagonally opposite the foot of the right rail: the supporting line + "one community, living across four countries — and growing." A short orange hairline (left-aligned) sits above it. *(~12%, low-left)*
6. **CTA + QR** — **bottom-left**, on the content side: lowercase "discover what's near you →" with the inline QR (~160–180px, dark-module SVG on cream) beside or below it and "arivela.co" with the QR. Bottom-right stays open cream (the rail's last node, `Tulum`, sits in that lower-right quadrant, so the corner isn't dead — it's the rail flowing off). *(~10%, bottom-left)*

*Reading path (≈5s): wordmark/eyebrow top-left → italic hero ranged left → eye drifts right across the open field and catches the descending city rail → low-left payoff "one community… and growing" → discover what's near you. The diagonal (top-left hero → bottom-right last city) is felt, not labelled.*

### BACK (hosts) — content-right, open-field-left, area-rail down the left edge (mirrored)

1. **Masthead / wordmark** — **top-right**, flush to the right safe-margin (mirroring the front's top-left). `ARIVELA` with the 2px **olive** accent rule right-aligned beneath; eyebrow `FOR THE PEOPLE WHO HOLD SPACE` in olive, right-flush. *(top band, ~10%)*
2. **Hero zone** — the Lora-italic host hero **ranged hard right**, hugging the right edge ("be found — / then left to teach."), intro line beneath, also right. The **negative-space cream field opens to the left**, holding the top of the left area rail. *(~20%)*
3. **Art zone (photo-or-illustration)** — a **tall off-center frame** placed right-of-axis and **bleeding off the RIGHT edge** (mirror of the front), top under the hero, left edge stopping at the off-center axis so the left field stays open. Smaller than the front's (the back is value-block heavy). *(~22%)*
4. **The area rail (mirrored)** — the five cities run **down the LEFT safe-margin as a descending staircase the opposite way**: `Rishikesh` upper-left, then `Ubud`, `London`, `Berlin`, `Tulum` stepping down toward lower-left, small Lora caps with tiny **olive** node-dots + faint hairline. Same scattered-not-ruled treatment. Switching edges (left here, right on front) is the deliberate asymmetry tell. *(left margin column, ~top-22%→bottom-28%)*
5. **Value block (the four props)** — the **centre of gravity on this side**, set as a **right-anchored, left-staggered list**: four rows ranged right against the off-center axis, each row a Lora-medium label + one Inter line + a small olive node-dot, the rows stepping a touch leftward as they descend so the block reads as a gentle diagonal rather than a centred column. Generous row spacing, thin `#E8E4DC` hairlines between. Prop (d) "coming soon" is the last, quietest row with the thin `SOON` chip. *(~30%, right side)*
6. **CTA + QR** — **bottom-right**, on the content side: "create your host profile →" + inline QR (~160–180px, dark/olive-module on cream) + "arivela.co". Bottom-left stays open cream where the rail's last city `Tulum` sits — the rail flowing off the lower-left corner. *(~10%, bottom-right)*

**Shared invariants (so photo & duotone converge, and front & back read as one object):** same off-center axis logic (mirrored 62%↔38%), same edge-anchored descending area-rail (right on front, left on back), same diagonal counter-movement, same art-bleeds-off-the-outer-edge rule, same masthead/CTA/QR geometry flipped across the gutter, same hairline color and node-dot motif. The **only** intended differences front↔back: which edge holds the content vs. the open field, the rail's edge and step direction, and the accent (orange→olive). Front is hero-and-field heavy (the feeling); back is value-block heavy (the offer).

## 5. Photo art-direction + Freepik shotlist

Encode the brand photography rules in every frame: **real people, not models**; diverse bodies and ages; calm focus and quiet joy (no ecstatic clichés, no guru-on-pedestal, no perfect-pose hero); natural window light and warm neutrals matching the palette; **lots of negative space** (essential here — the frame must hold the off-center composition's open field); observed, not staged. **Avoid** altars, incense, mandalas, sacred geometry, festival aesthetics. Each photo slot prints as an **empty styled frame** (soft 6–8px radius, hairline `#E8E4DC` border or subtle cream-tinted fill) containing its printed art-direction text + Freepik cue — no stand-in images. Because the frame **bleeds off the outer edge** and is **off-center**, direct the photographer's negative space to fall toward the axis/open-field side so the headline and rail never fight the image.

### FRONT (guests) — the left-bleeding off-center frame

- **Brief:** One tall, calm, atmospheric image anchored **low-left and bleeding off the left edge** — a sense of a real person *somewhere in the world*, with **the negative space falling to the right** (toward the open field and the hero). Think a single person or a small pair in a warm, lived-in interior near a window, off to one side of the frame, lots of air on the other side. Not a destination postcard; a feeling of "someone real, somewhere out there, that you could belong with." The off-center subject must leave the **right half of the frame quiet** so the composition's open field continues into the photo.
  - *Freepik:* "person by window warm natural light negative space", "diverse people candid documentary warm tones minimal", "calm interior single figure soft daylight lots of space".
- *(Optional small inset, if the layout wants a second human note tucked into the open field — kept tiny so one focal moment holds):* two people in quiet conversation after a session, side light, no eye-contact-to-camera.
  - *Freepik:* "two people quiet conversation studio natural light", "yoga studio candid real people diverse unposed".

### BACK (hosts) — the right-bleeding off-center frame

- **Brief:** A host *in their element*, understated, anchored **low-right and bleeding off the right edge**, with **negative space falling to the left** (toward the open field and the value block). A teacher or facilitator in their own space — a studio, a retreat home, a training room — mid-ordinary-moment, off to one side of the frame, not performing. Warm window light, a real space with a little honest wear, calm competence. Read it as "a real person who holds space here," not a brand ambassador. The left portion of the frame stays quiet so the open field flows into the image.
  - *Freepik:* "yoga teacher own studio natural light candid off center", "facilitator preparing space warm neutral documentary negative space", "wellness host real person calm not posed minimal interior".
- *(Optional small inset near the "add your places" prop, tucked into the open left field):* a quiet detail of a *place* — an empty studio floor, mats rolled, a retreat porch — warm, lived-in, lots of air.
  - *Freepik:* "empty yoga studio warm light minimal negative space", "retreat space interior natural light calm", "training room wooden floor soft daylight".

## 6. Duotone illustration direction

Hand-authored **inline SVG**, two-tone, organic flowing lines, rounded soft geometry, **no facial features**, gender-neutral silhouettes, lots of breathing room. Optional very-low-opacity `feTurbulence` grain. No drop shadows, no faux-3D, no mandalas/chakras. The motif language for this concept is **the off-center field and the edge-strung places** — the duotone must obey the *same asymmetric grid*: the illustration is **anchored off-center and bleeds off the outer edge**, the open field stays mostly empty, and the **five areas read as duotone art at the margin rail** (soft node-dots strung diagonally down the edge), never as a centred ring.

### FRONT (guests) — tones: **cream `#F9F6EF` + orange `#C8702A`** (warm, inviting)

A single abstract human figure rendered as one continuous orange **breath-curve line**, placed **low-left and clipped off the left edge** (off-center, never centred), mid-gentle-movement and *opening toward the right* — the line leaving the body unspools rightward and **becomes the rail of place-dots** that climbs/steps along the right margin: five soft orange nodes (Rishikesh · Ubud · London · Berlin · Tulum) descending the right edge, the topmost largest, connected by one calm flowing line. The **open cream field between the figure (left) and the place-rail (right)** is the deliberate negative space — the figure on one edge, the world strung down the other edge, the breath connecting them across the empty middle. A faint cream tint (`#FAF3EA`) may wash the figure's clipped block. No centred constellation — the places live **off-center, at the right margin**, as a descending duotone rail.

### BACK (hosts) — tones: **cream `#F9F6EF` + olive `#6B6E45`** (grounded, credible)

The **mirror** composition: a single abstract figure in a calm, rooted "holding space" stance rendered as one confident olive line, placed **low-right and clipped off the right edge** (off-center). The line that leaves the figure unspools **leftward** and becomes the **left-margin rail of five olive place-dots** descending the left edge — but here the rail's lines lean *toward* the host figure, "the community moves toward you; you become findable." The **open cream field on the left**, between the descending place-rail and the centre, holds the four value props (in the page; the illustration leaves that field clear). A light olive-tint (`#F5F6F0`) may wash the figure's clipped block; the "coming soon" idea reads as one faint, lighter olive node set slightly apart at the rail's foot so it recedes. Same off-center grid, same edge-strung-areas logic, mirrored — so the duotone pair is unmistakably one set and unmistakably *off the center*.

**Shared skeleton:** identical off-center axis (mirrored), identical "figure clipped off the outer edge + areas as a descending margin rail" logic, identical node-dot motif and line weight; only the accent tone (orange→olive), the figure's edge (left→right), the rail's edge (right→left), and the line's direction (opening out vs. drawing in) change. That is what makes both the photo pair and the duotone pair read as one coherent, deliberately asymmetric set.

## 7. Colour emphasis

- **Guests (FRONT) — warm / orange-forward.** Page cream `#F9F6EF` base; orange `#C8702A` carries the eyebrow, the left-aligned accent rule under the wordmark, the **right-edge area rail's node-dots and connecting line**, the supporting-line hairline, and the CTA. Warm-cream `#FFF6E9` / `#FAF3EA` may tint the off-center art block. Olive appears only as a quiet grounding whisper (a single node-dot or hairline) so the two sides stay a related pair. Mood: warmth, invitation, "a place for you, wherever you are."
- **Hosts (BACK) — grounded / olive-forward.** Same cream base; olive `#6B6E45` carries the eyebrow, the right-aligned accent rule, the **left-edge area rail's node-dots and line**, the value-row markers, and the value-block hairlines. The CTA keeps the brand **orange `#C8702A`** (CTAs are the one place orange always leads, for consistency and warm contrast against the olive field) — the single loud-warm note on the grounded back is the one action we want the host to take. Mood: trust, depth, "you belong here too — at your own edge of the map."
- **The justified link:** orange = the *invitation in* and the warm scatter of places (guests); olive = the *ground you stand on* and the steady scatter of places (hosts). Each side leads with its own accent but borrows one touch of the other, and the **off-center area-rail in the accent colour** is what visually ties the spine across both edges of the flyer — one community, strung along the margins, two moods.
